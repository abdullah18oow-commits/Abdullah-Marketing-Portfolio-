A NUTRIFACTOR SUNDION CASE STUDY Three Years Slower? Role   Concept · Script · Set direction · Talent direction · Edit direction Brand   Nutrifactor     Product   Sundion (Vitamin D3, 200,000IU) Format   Explainer video, Instagram + paid ads A podcast-style explainer that turns one study into a simple story. TAKE 2 · THE BRIEF Everyone has heard “take your Vitamin D”, and most people scroll past it. A recent Harvard study on cellular aging gave us a reason to talk about it again. My job was to use that study to earn attention, explain it without overclaiming, and lead viewers to Sundion, a high-potency Vitamin D3, in a feed that gives you about two seconds. TAKE 3 · THE IDEA Start with the headline. Then tell the truth about it. Instead of a typical product pitch, I built a mini podcast: a host in an armchair with a mic, explaining one study in plain language. Science visuals do the proving, and the script corrects the hype itself: Vitamin D does not make you three years younger. That honesty is what makes the product part believable. Credibility was the whole job. TAKE 4 · THE SCRIPT, CONDENSED BEAT WHAT HAPPENS WHY IT’S THERE The hook A recent Harvard study: people taking Vitamin D3 regularly showed cellular aging almost 3 years slower. A specific, surprising number opens the loop, backed by the article on screen The reality check “This doesn’t mean Vitamin D makes you 3 years younger.” Researchers studied telomeres, an aging marker inside cells. Corrects the hype before viewers do, which builds trust The bigger picture Vitamin D3 is already known to support bones, muscles, immunity and mood. Links a headline study to benefits people already care about The problem Many people are Vitamin D deficient and don’t even know it. Moves from curiosity to “this could be me” The product Sundion: high-potency 200,000IU Vitamin D3 that helps restore levels and supports calcium absorption. The product arrives as the answer, only after the story The close To support bone health, immunity and healthy aging, start using Vit D and try Nutrifactor Sundion. One clear call to action, tied to three benefits A note on claims:  the script uses “help” and “support” throughout, and says outright that Vitamin D does not make you younger. [Optional: add who reviewed the claims.] TAKE 5 · BEHIND THE DESK The set.  A podcast corner: armchair, studio mic, a plant and side table on a clean off-white backdrop, framed vertically for the feed The performance.  A long, spoken-style Urdu-English script, delivered like a conversation with open-hand gestures rather than read out. TAKE 6 · THE PROOF LAYER The article.  The Harvard Gazette piece sits behind the host as the study is introduced, so the claim has a source on screen. The diagram.  A labelled chromosome shows the telomere, so “aging marker” becomes something you can see. The result.  A split-face visual reads “3 years slower cellular aging”, balanced by the script’s reality check. Also on screen:  a “200,000IU of Vit.D3” title for the product, and a numbered list (Bone Health, Immunity, Healthy Aging) for the close. TAKE 7 · OUT IN THE WORLD WHERE IT RAN Posted on Nutrifactor’s Instagram, then handed to the media buying team to run as paid ads. BUILT FOR THE FEED A hook with a number and a headline in the first seconds, on-screen text that carries each point, and one clear CTA. NEXT TIME  Would try to make it more interesting by adding an analogy to explain things in a easier fashion.
