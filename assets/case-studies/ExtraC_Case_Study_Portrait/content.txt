A CELLCENSE EXTRA-C SERUM CASE STUDY
Smudge. Wipe. Spotless.
Role   Concept · Script · Set direction · Talent direction · Edit direction
Brand   Cellcense by Nutrifactor     Product   Extra-C Serum
Format   Short product video, Instagram + paid ads

TAKE 2 · THE BRIEF
Cellcense Extra-C Serum is a Vitamin C brightening serum, and this video has just one line of script. That meant the visual had to do the selling. My job was to make “spotless” something viewers see in the first seconds, and to make a short, simple product video fun enough to watch to the end.

TAKE 3 · THE IDEA
Show “spotless” before you say it.
The video opens on a black smudge on the “screen”, the kind everyone has on their phone. A drop of serum wipes it clean, so the benefit is demonstrated before anyone says it. Then the product name flies in and bonks the host on the head, which turns the title into a character and keeps the tone light.
I skipped a benefits voiceover. With one line of script, the gag is the message.

TAKE 4 · THE VIDEO, BEAT BY BEAT
BEAT | WHAT HAPPENS | WHY IT’S THERE
The entrance | The host walks into frame. | Movement in the first second
The smudge | A black smudge sits on the “screen”, like on a phone display. | Viewers instinctively check their own screen, a perfect pattern interrupt
The wipe | She puts a drop of serum on the smudge and wipes it away, like cleaning a mirror. | “Spotless” is shown, not claimed
The hit | “Extra-C Serum” flies in from outside the frame and hits her on the head. | A slapstick gag that makes the title a character
The wait | She points at the text to wait a second, sits down, then gestures it back in. | She stays in control, and it resets the frame for the pitch
The line | “Cellcense Extra-C Serum, for smooth, spotless and glowing skin.” | One short line that echoes the wipe from the opening
A note on claims: the demo wipes a screen, not skin, so the visual makes no skin claim. The benefit is stated once, in the closing line.

TAKE 5 · BEHIND THE SMUDGE
The set. A bright, warm corner with an arched mirror, palms, a teal armchair and window light. The host’s cream outfit keeps it clean, and the orange titles echo the bottle.
The camera. The wipe is played to the lens, so the camera glass becomes the smudged screen.
The performance. Comic timing: the hit, the look up at the text, the “wait a second” point.
The edit. The text flies in from off-screen and out again, timed to her gestures.

TAKE 6 · OUT IN THE WORLD
WHERE IT RAN
Posted on Nutrifactor’s Instagram, then handed to the media buying team to run as paid ads.
BUILT FOR THE FEED
A visual gag in the first second, a product name on screen that works with the sound off, and one short benefit line.
